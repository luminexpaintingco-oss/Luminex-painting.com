Questions to Ask Before Hiring Interior Painters in Concord, NC

Before comparing painting estimates, make sure you know what is included, what happens before painting starts, and how the finished work will be reviewed.

November 20, 2026 · Luminex Painting Co.

Hiring someone to repaint a bedroom, hallway or several rooms is easier when you ask specific questions—not just “How much?” Two proposals can describe very different projects. This checklist helps Concord homeowners compare interior painting services on the same basis and avoid surprises.

Start here: Write down the rooms and surfaces you want painted, plus any dents, stains, peeling or color changes. Bring that list to each estimate.

1. Exactly which surfaces are included?

Ask whether the proposal covers walls only or also ceilings, baseboards, trim, door frames and interior doors. Clarify closets, stairwells, accent walls and any spaces you are intentionally leaving out. “Paint the bedroom” can mean different things to different people.

2. What surface preparation is part of the estimate?

Point out nail holes, small cracks, scuffs, rough patches and old repairs. Ask which will be patched, sanded, caulked or primed, and whether larger repairs need a separate decision. If paint is peeling or there is water staining, ask how the cause will be evaluated before coating over it. Active leaks or moisture problems need to be corrected first.

3. Which paint products and finishes will be used?

Ask for the proposed product line and sheen for each surface, rather than only a color name. A finish appropriate for a quiet bedroom may not be the same one you prefer in a busy hallway. Consider cleaning needs, lighting and visible wall imperfections. See our interior paint finish guide for a practical comparison.

4. Who buys the paint and how are colors confirmed?

Confirm whether materials are included in the estimate, who purchases them and when final colors must be chosen. Ask how color codes, brands and finishes will be documented. If you are changing from a dark color to a light one, discuss whether a primer or additional coats may be needed. Ask how a change after approval would affect the scope.

5. What does the schedule actually look like?

Ask for the anticipated start window, expected workdays, room access needs and how delays will be communicated. Drying between coats depends on the product, ventilation and conditions; a room being dry to the touch does not necessarily mean the coating has fully cured. Discuss when furniture can be moved back and when newly painted surfaces can be cleaned.

6. How will the home be protected and cleaned up?

Ask about floor coverings, furniture protection, masking, dust management, daily cleanup and where materials will be stored. If your family will be home during painting, mention pets, children, work-from-home spaces and any rooms that must stay usable. Review our home preparation checklist before the appointment.

7. Who will perform the work and how is it coordinated?

Ask who will be on site, who is responsible for communicating changes and who to contact if a question comes up. If subcontractors are involved, ask how the company coordinates their work and who remains accountable for the agreed scope. You can also ask for applicable insurance information and verify any licensing or registration requirements that actually apply to the proposed work rather than assuming every painting job requires the same credentials.

8. What will the written estimate show?

Look for a clear description of rooms, surfaces, prep, paint materials, exclusions, payment terms and anticipated scheduling. Ask what could cause a change order and whether any added work requires your approval first. A low number is difficult to evaluate if it leaves out ceilings, repairs or doors that another estimate includes. For more context, read the Concord interior painting cost guide and our bedroom estimate factors.

9. How will you review the finished work?

Ask when you can walk through the rooms, how any missed areas or concerns should be reported, and what cleanup is included before completion. Confirm any workmanship terms in writing if offered, rather than assuming every contractor provides identical coverage. Keep a copy of the final scope and agreed changes.

10. What happens if the project changes?

Maybe you decide to include the ceiling after work starts, or a damaged section needs more preparation than expected. Ask how additional work will be priced, approved and scheduled. A simple written change process helps both sides know what is being done.

A short checklist to bring to your estimate

  • List every room and specify walls, ceilings, trim, baseboards and doors.
  • Photograph or point out repairs and stains; mention moisture concerns.
  • Choose preliminary colors and note preferred finishes.
  • Ask what protection, cleanup and final walkthrough include.
  • Compare written proposals using the same scope, not just the total.

Questions homeowners often ask

Should I get more than one interior painting estimate?

Comparing estimates can be useful, especially for a larger project. Make sure each painter is quoting the same rooms, surfaces and prep before comparing totals.

Should I choose colors before requesting an estimate?

You can start with ideas. Tell the painter about major color changes and ask when the final selections are needed, since color and product choices can affect preparation and coverage.

Is the lowest quote always the best value?

Not necessarily. Review what is included, the materials proposed, preparation, schedule, communication and written terms. A lower quote may simply cover fewer surfaces or repairs.
