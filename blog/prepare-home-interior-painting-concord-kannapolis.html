How to prepare your home for interior painting

A little planning before painting day can make the process smoother. Here is what homeowners can do ahead of time—and what to discuss with their painting contractor first.

By Luminex Painting Co. · November 7, 2026 · Homeowner guide

Planning to repaint a bedroom, living room or several rooms in your home? Whether you live in Concord or Kannapolis, knowing how to prepare for interior painting helps you make clear decisions about the scope, schedule and access to each space.

Some preparation is a homeowner's responsibility, and some may be included in a professional painter's quote. Always confirm the division of work in your written estimate rather than assuming every task is included.

1. Decide which rooms and surfaces need painting

Make a simple room-by-room list. For example: “living room walls only,” “two bedroom walls and ceilings,” or “hallway walls, trim and interior doors.” It helps to distinguish between walls, ceilings, baseboards, window trim, doors and frames because they can require different preparation and painting steps.

If you're unsure whether a repair area belongs in the estimate, point it out during your walkthrough. The more complete the initial scope, the easier it is to avoid surprises later.

2. Clear small items and protect valuables

Before work starts, remove loose objects from shelves and surfaces near the areas being painted. Take down fragile decor, personal items, and anything you would rather store safely away from the work zone. Ask your painter whether furniture moving and floor covering are included in the quoted scope.

  • Put away breakable items, electronics, and valuables in the affected rooms.
  • Remove small furniture or accessories where practical.
  • Make pathways to the rooms accessible for people and equipment.
  • Discuss large or heavy furniture with the contractor rather than attempting to move it yourself.

3. Identify holes, cracks, stains and other wall concerns

Walk around each room in good light. Look for nail holes, damaged drywall, peeling paint, water marks, cracks and uneven areas. Share these concerns during the estimate so everyone understands which repairs will be made before painting and whether any specialist repair is needed.

Do not paint over an active leak or ongoing moisture problem. The source should be corrected and the surface assessed before finishing work begins. For homes with older coatings, avoid disturbing paint that may contain lead; the U.S. Environmental Protection Agency has specific lead-safe renovation rules for many pre-1978 homes.

Tip: Take a few photos of damaged areas before your estimate. Pictures can help your painter understand the condition of the walls, but an in-person inspection may still be necessary.

4. Choose colors—or narrow your options

You do not have to know your final paint color before you request an estimate, but it is helpful to have a few ideas ready. Consider how each room looks in daylight and under its usual evening lighting. If you plan a big color change or want different colors on multiple walls, mention that when discussing the scope.

Keep the paint brand, color name and code together if you already have a sample. A small test patch or sample board can help you compare colors in your own space before committing to the entire room.

5. Plan for kids, pets and everyday access

Discuss the work schedule and which rooms will be temporarily unavailable. Keep children and pets away from active work areas, equipment and paint products. Plan how you will access bathrooms, bedrooms and essential household items while a room is being prepared and painted.

Talk with your painter about ventilation, odors and any special household needs. Product instructions and the condition of your home influence when painted spaces can be used normally again.

6. Review the written painting estimate together

A useful estimate makes the project boundaries clear. Before you accept it, check the rooms and surfaces, preparation tasks, paint materials, number of finish coats where specified, furniture arrangements, start dates and any exclusions. If a particular ceiling, door or closet isn't included, it should be easy to identify that from the proposal.

Ask how changes will be priced if you decide to add another room after work begins. This is especially helpful when coordinating a multi-room interior painting project.

7. Do one final walk-through before painting day

Confirm access to the property, where supplies may be staged, which items should remain untouched, and the best phone number for reaching you if a question comes up. Take a few before photos for your own records. These are also useful for comparing the space when the work is complete.

A quick homeowner checklist

  • List the rooms and exact surfaces to be painted.
  • Identify wall repairs, stains and damaged areas to discuss.
  • Choose colors or prepare a shortlist of samples.
  • Put away valuables, small accessories and fragile items.
  • Discuss large furniture and floor protection with the painter.
  • Make a plan for pets, children and access to essential rooms.
  • Review the written scope, exclusions and scheduling details.

Frequently asked questions

Do I need to move all my furniture before painters arrive?

Not necessarily. The answer depends on the contractor and the agreed scope. Ask which items you should move and whether moving or covering larger furniture is part of the estimate.

Should I patch nail holes before hiring a painter?

Show the painter the holes first. Minor filling and sanding may be part of the preparation scope, but larger repairs may need separate attention. Confirm what is included in writing.

Can I request an estimate before choosing my paint colors?

Yes. You can discuss the rooms and surfaces first. If you expect major color changes or several accent colors, mention them during the estimate so the proposed work reflects your plans.
